# Independent NXDN complete-frame reference: construction gate passed

Arithmetic construction and a separately compiled primary encoder agree on all
**864 output bytes** for the nine registered clear conventional frames, before
and after channel whitening. All **24 known voice-word intervals** occupy their
registered slots, including both directions of FACCH half stealing. This is a
complete-frame reference for the next receiver-routing experiment, not evidence
that the receiver accepts a call, produces correct speech, or decodes faster.

## Independent source and the corrected primary oracle

Primary encoding uses pinned [MMDVM-Host](https://github.com/g4klx/MMDVM-Host/tree/590c531391dfd3146073afbc3956f70d42c62a46):
real LICH, SACCH, FACCH1, convolution, CRC, audio, Golay and synchronization
functions. The 48-byte whitening declaration comes from its
[NXDNControl.cpp](https://github.com/g4klx/MMDVM-Host/blob/590c531391dfd3146073afbc3956f70d42c62a46/NXDNControl.cpp).
Source words retain the pinned [NXDNClients announcement asset](https://github.com/g4klx/NXDNClients/blob/8950677e9876e577fb87b955cfa93bacd059209d/NXDNGateway/Audio/en_GB.nxdn)
lineage. Original files, Git blob identities and license notices are archived.

The independently registered five changes apply only to private primary source
copies: initialize four encoding temporaries and bound the FACCH puncture-list
index after the final puncture. The original
[FACCH implementation](https://github.com/g4klx/MMDVM-Host/blob/590c531391dfd3146073afbc3956f70d42c62a46/NXDNFACCH1.cpp)
would otherwise read beyond its 48-entry table for the final coded bits.
Exact replacement counts and retained diffs prove the limited copy changes;
no decoder method or product source was patched in this stage.

Arithmetic construction uses the hash-frozen independent control and voice
primitives, plus a generated whitening recurrence. It does not consult the
primary encoding tables or receiver output. The checker imports neither encoder:
it reconstructs original source fields, validates exact identities and every
voice interval, and compares all output bytes. These implementations share
protocol knowledge; agreement is not formal standards certification.
